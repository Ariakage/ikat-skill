# Bound Warp — design philosophy

ikat 是一种古老的织法：经纱在织前就按图案扎染定位，织成后图案
自带羽状晕边——每一根线在被绑定之前就已决定了自己在整体中的位置。
这正是一个确定性索引的精神写照：结构不是事后推断出来的，而是
在测量发生的那一刻就被绑定的。

The visual language is textile mathematics: columns of yarns bound
into chevron registers, feathered dye edges rendered as deliberate
misalignments that are anything but accidental. Space behaves like a
loom — the ground is a deep indigo warp, quiet and dense, against
which the bound pattern registers rise like woven bands. Form is
repetition with intent: a single motif, iterated with the patience of
a craft that measures twice and binds once, accumulating into rhythm.

Color is restrained to three registers — indigo ground, warm
vermillion weft, and undyed natural thread — because a dye-house
palette gains authority from scarcity. Each hue is placed with
meticulous precision, as if mixed by hand and tested against the cloth
a dozen times. Nothing shouts; the pattern earns attention through
density and the unmistakable sign of countless careful decisions.

Embedded in the weave, almost subliminal, is a second structure: a
lattice of circles and hairline edges — the graph the textile was
secretly encoding all along. It should feel discovered rather than
drawn, the way a pattern in a master weaver's cloth suddenly resolves
into meaning after sustained looking. This double-read is the soul of
the piece: woven ornament for the passing eye, architecture for the
attentive.

Typography is sparse and clinical — a single wordmark, small
mono-spaced annotations floating like a weaver's tally marks beside
the work. Text never decorates; it documents. The whole composition
must read as the painstaking product of someone at the top of their
craft — every feathered edge intentional, every register aligned to a
grid invisible but felt, the kind of artifact that rewards the second
and third look.
